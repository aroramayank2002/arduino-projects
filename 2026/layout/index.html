Sheet & wiring

Why A3, how the pins were allocated, and the one rule that saves the board.

Why A3, not A4

How the pins fall out

  • Reserved, untouched: 0 / 1 are Serial over USB — leave them free or the Serial Monitor fights the wiring.
  • I²C on 20 / 21 carries three parts on one pair: BH1750 0x23, OLED 0x3C, PCA9685 0x40. No address collisions, so they share the rail.
  • Hardware SPI on 50–52 serves the TFT; its chip-select and control lines sit on 10 / 9 / 8, matching jatin/128_160_TFT_MODULE.
  • Interrupt pins 18 / 19 go to the rotary encoder, the only part that can miss steps if polled.
  • PWM for motion: servos on 4 / 6 / 7, L298N enables on 2 / 3. Pins 44–46 are deliberately left clear — the Servo library claims Timer5 and kills analogWrite on them.
  • The high block 22–49 takes everything that is just on or off: LEDs, switches, the reed, the touch pad, the thermocouple's soft-SPI.

Build rules

Current. Servos and the L298N never draw through the Mega's 5 V pin. They take PS1, the buck converter, on its own pair, with grounds commoned once at TB1. A stalling SG90 pulls close to an amp; the Mega's regulator gives up long before that.

Everything is 5 V. One controller means no level shifting anywhere on the panel — that is the reason to standardise on the Mega for a teaching kit, and the reason its 54 pins matter.

Panel & hardware

  1. 3 mm MDF or acrylic at 420 × 297, edges broken R3.
  2. Solid outline = module on M3 × 6 standoffs, drill ⌀3.2 at the four marks.
  3. Dashed inner window = cut through: displays, switches, jacks, encoder shaft, LEDs, buzzer, touch pad.
  4. Print at 100 %, spray-mount to the stock, drill through, peel. Verify a few footprints with calipers first — vendors vary a millimetre or two.
